## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored for this product, unmistakably — and it survives the hard version of the test.** The composition cannot be lifted onto another product without breaking, because it is made of things that only mean something here. The title block is a real `<dl>` of `ISSUED / REVISED / EXTENT / BY / SUBJECT / ALSO IN` that *omits* rows rather than blanking them (`layouts/_partials/title-block.html:23`, `:46-62`) — a data structure, not a caption style. The zone rail's marks carry real hrefs at measured per-section offsets, and the home page renders no rail at all with the frame closing the column, so the "earn the metaphor or drop it" rule is genuinely enforced (`20-layout.css:209`). Zero `box-shadow` anywhere; the line-weight system is two widths crossed with three inks, as documented.

The claim fails in the one place it leans hardest. PRODUCT.md's lead differentiator is the rail — "a real section index… the section being read lit." It is a real index, and it is set at `--step--2` (13px) in `--meta-soft`, in a 40px gutter, with a 20×13px hit box, while the ToC carrying the same information in words ships **closed** (`exampleSite/hugo.toml:167`). The theme's own cover illustration draws the rail bigger, bolder and in its own ruled column than the implementation does. **The metaphor is structure; the structure is set at the volume of a footnote.** That gap is the through-line of most findings below.

**Deterministic scan: 0 findings — and that number is worth nothing.** `detect.mjs --json layouts` exited 0, but printed `DEGRADED — HTML parser modules unavailable (htmlparser2, css-select, css-tree, domutils)`, falling back to regex. Assessment B verified the degradation empirically: fed a scratch file containing a box-shadow, a 13px radius, `opacity: 0.6`, a purple/pink gradient, a 96px h1, an unaltered `<img>` and an empty `<button>`, the fallback caught **1 of 9**. It is also structurally blind here — `layouts/*.html` are Hugo templates full of `{{ }}`, which the static-HTML engine cannot parse into a DOM at all. Read the zero as "scan degraded", never as "clean". Installing `htmlparser2 css-select css-tree domutils puppeteer` under `.claude/skills/impeccable/scripts/` would restore full fidelity and unlock URL-mode scanning, which would have caught several findings below automatically.

**Visual overlays: injection succeeded on all five routes**, in both schemes and both viewports. The in-page detector returned three findings, and all three are false positives, which is itself a result:
- `cramped-padding` on `div.table-wrap` — the wrapper is a scroll container by design; its inset comes from the cells one rule down (`50-single.css:594`). Padding the wrapper would float the border off the table edge, which is exactly the flush drawn-frame look.
- `em-dash-overuse` (9 in body) — house voice, in `exampleSite/` prose, not in `layouts/`.
- `cramped-padding` 4px on tag-cloud `<a>` — a 0.5px shortfall on the fine-pointer path, raised to `--tap-target` under `@media (pointer: coarse)` (`40-list.css:316-320`), which is the documented trade.

Assessment B also discarded its own first contrast pass: toggling `data-theme` in-page returned stale `light-dark()` values (a dark `--text` read against a light `--bg`, ratio 1.09). Re-run with clean per-scheme loads, every one of those disappeared.

## Overall Impression

This is a theme that has done the hard part and under-sold it. The structural work is genuine and unusually disciplined — the conditional title-block rows, the uniform focus treatment across sixteen consecutive tab stops, the landmark structure, the contrast floor that holds to the exact number DESIGN.md claims. What lets it down is not taste but a handful of specific places where a documented intention did not reach the pixels: an anchor rule trapped inside a media query, a button parked on top of the code it copies, a navigation system rendered too quietly to navigate by, and a manifest link pointing at a file that does not exist.

The single biggest opportunity: **make the rail as loud as the positioning statement says it is.** Every other finding is a bug; that one is the design decision the product hangs on.

Worth stating plainly, because it reframes the rest: the two commitments PRODUCT.md calls binding are both script-checkable, and both hold. Contrast: every text token clears 4.5:1 in both schemes, measured minimum **4.87:1** — matching DESIGN.md:73's stated figure exactly, so that number is verified rather than asserted. Zero elements anywhere dim text with `opacity`. Exactly one `h1` per route, zero heading-level skips, zero empty accessible names, zero missing `alt`, zero horizontal overflow at 390px, zero uncaught exceptions. Every P0 and P1 below is a property no script was watching.

## What's Working

1. **The title block is the real thing, and it is the reason to adopt this theme.** Not because it looks drawn, but because `title-block.html:23` withholds `REVISED` unless `Lastmod` exceeds `Date` by more than 24 hours, and `:46-62` withhold `SUBJECT` and `ALSO IN` when there is nothing to say. On a drawing, an empty field means "not applicable" and a missing one means "not recorded" — the template encodes that distinction. That is what makes it read as an instrument rather than a skin.

2. **The focus treatment is uniform across sixteen consecutive tab stops.** Skip link, brand, four nav items, language switch, theme button, four zone marks, two breadcrumbs, two tag links — every one returns exactly `2px solid rgb(138, 82, 0)` at `4px` offset. Not one control falls back to the UA ring or doubles up. The search field's `outline-offset: 0` (`60-search.css:22-25`) is a deliberate exception so the outline lands *on* the border instead of drawing a second concentric ring. Most design systems never reach that level.

3. **Landmark and role structure is quietly excellent.** `NAV[aria-label="Primary"]`, `"Zones"`, `"Breadcrumb"`, `"Posts"`; tables wrapped in `role="region"`; all five alerts as `role="note"`. A screen-reader user gets a better map of this page than a sighted user does — which is unusual, and is also the seed of the recall problem below.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The copy button covers the code on every touch device
**What.** `50-single.css:693-695` sets `.js .copy-code { opacity: 1; min-height: var(--tap-target) }` under `@media (pointer: coarse)`. With `position: absolute; top/right: var(--space-3xs)` (`:653-660`) and a `<pre>` carrying only 12px top padding at 390px, the result is a permanently visible **51×44px button at x=318** over the first two lines of every code block, where text runs to x=361. Measured on all three blocks of `/en/posts/code-and-terminal-output/` at 390×844. Worse: block 2 has `scrollWidth 721` vs `clientWidth 356`, and the button is positioned against `.highlight`, which does not scroll — so it masks a 51px column of code no matter how far you scroll sideways. The back-to-top button lands on the same blocks.
**Why it matters.** PRODUCT.md Principle 1 is "the reading scene is code, not prose", and success is defined as working through a post full of commands intact. The theme's stated reason to exist, broken on the primary reading device, on the demo post named for it.
**Fix.** Under `pointer: coarse`, move the button out of the code's box — add `padding-top` to `pre` equal to the button height, or dock it to a ruled strip above the block, which is also more drawing-like than a floating chip. Keep desktop hover behaviour as-is.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P0] `scroll-margin-top` is trapped inside `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`
**What.** `10-base.css:95-104` puts `:target { scroll-margin-top: var(--space-3xl) }` inside the same media block as `scroll-behavior: smooth`. Verified in source and measured: with `reduce`, `scrollMarginTop` computes to **`0px`** and `/en/posts/reading-a-sheet/#alerts` lands the heading at **top 34px under a 57px header** — invisible. With `no-preference` it computes to `64px` and lands cleanly.
**Why it matters.** Every zone-rail click, every ToC link, every revision-flag reference and every deep link from a search engine lands under the header, for exactly the users who set an accessibility preference. The comment on line 100 documents an intent the code honours for half the audience. It also silently defeats the rail, the product's headline feature.
**Fix.** Hoist the `:target` rule out of the media query; leave only `scroll-behavior: smooth` inside. One line.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The rail is the only wayfinding, and on a phone there is none at all
**What.** On mobile the rail is removed by design (`20-layout.css:501-515`, correct), and the ToC that DESIGN.md nominates as its replacement ships **closed** (`exampleSite/hugo.toml:167`), contradicting the theme's own template comment at `_partials/toc.html:1-4` ("Open by default on a long sheet, since a reader who arrived from a search engine is usually looking for one section"). Verified: `toc.open === false` at 390×844 on a **4898px-tall** page — roughly six phone screens with zero wayfinding. On desktop the rail exists but its lit state was off-screen or under the header at 3 of 5 sampled offsets, and each mark is a 20×13px letter with no visible section name.
**Why it matters.** The mode is Read; comprehension of a long structured document is the entire job. Right now the reader's position is untracked on desktop and unknowable on mobile.
**Fix.** Three parts: (a) flip `TocOpen` to `true` in the demo so the showcase demonstrates the theme's own stated model; (b) make the lit zone survive being scrolled past — pin the current mark below the header while its section is active, or extend the tick into a full-height bracket spanning the section so the *region* is lit, not just the letter; (c) reveal the section title beside the letter on hover/focus so the rail stops being pure recall.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] `<link rel="manifest">` points at a file the theme never ships
**What.** `_partials/head-assets.html:73` emits unconditionally: `<link rel="manifest" href="{{ "site.webmanifest" | absURL }}">`. I confirmed `http://localhost:1313/site.webmanifest` returns **404**, on every route, in both schemes and both viewports. Chrome logs two console errors per page load. Every neighbouring asset link in that block is guarded by `{{- with site.Params.assets }}{{- with .favicon }}…`; the manifest alone is not.
**Why it matters.** This is the only console error the theme produces anywhere, and it fires on 100% of page loads of every site running the theme. It is also a promise broken in the other direction — the theme advertises PWA installability and does not deliver it. An evaluator with DevTools open sees a red error on the demo's first screen.
**Fix.** Guard it the way its neighbours are guarded, or ship a `site.webmanifest` and document the param in README.md. One or the other; the current state is the only wrong answer.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] The search page is the least-finished route in the product
**What.** Four defects on one screen at `/en/search/`: (a) the empty state for a no-match query is one 13px mono line, `Nothing found.`, over ~470px of blank graph paper — no suggestion, no reset, no way out — while the 404 solves the identical problem with two buttons plus a full LATEST index; (b) results carry **no meta line** at all, breaking DESIGN.md's "one invariant field grid" on the one page where telling five similar sheets apart matters most; (c) it is the only index route without breadcrumbs (`layouts/search.html:2-6` omits the partial that `list.html` and `taxonomy.html` include); (d) the native `✕` clear control renders in Chrome blue, the only unthemed browser surface on the site.
**Why it matters.** PRODUCT.md's secondary audience "decides within a screen or two". Search is where an evaluator tests whether their content is findable, and it is the route that looks least like the rest of the theme.
**Fix.** Reuse `post-card.html` for results so the field grid holds; give the empty state the 404's treatment — the exits already exist in the `<noscript>` fallback at `search.html:32-35` and are hidden from precisely the users who need them; add the breadcrumb partial; style `::-webkit-search-cancel-button` from the palette.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

## Minor Observations

- **The `<noscript>` fallback is better than the JS experience.** `search.html:30-36` gives non-JS users a note plus links to Posts and Tags. JS users who search for something with no matches get neither. The recovery path already exists in the template and is hidden from the people who need it.
- **Amber appears three times on one screen** at `/en/posts/reading-a-sheet/` — the `POSTS` nav underline, `.alert--tip`, and `.alert--important` — against DESIGN.md's "never doing more than one job on a screen at a time". The IMPORTANT box that *states* "Only one accent is ever live on a screen at a time" is itself one of the three. Per CLAUDE.md's own rule, the screenshot wins and DESIGN.md is stale here — but which way it should be reconciled is a design decision, not a defect fix.
- **The AI stamp's note overlays the title-block rows it annotates** — open on `/en/posts/multilingual-by-design/`, the 360px note covers the `EXTENT`, `BY` and `SUBJECT` values beside it.
- **The German nav leaves `TAGS` untranslated** beside `BEITRÄGE / ARCHIV / SUCHE`.
- **A figure caption is set in JetBrains Mono** ("The sheet, empty. Everything below is a field on it.") — prose in the face DESIGN.md reserves for "data and measurement only".
- **`/en/posts/` stamps "5 sheets" directly above a note reading "Nine posts across two languages".**
- **The 404's `LATEST` stamp carries no count** while the home page's `DRAWING OFFICE` stamp carries `5 sheets` — same component, one field short.
- **Search relevance is too loose to be informative**: `zone` returns all 5 of 5 indexed sheets. A query that matches everything is the same as no query, and with no meta line and no term highlighting there is nothing to rank by eye.
- **`/en/tags/` and `/en/search/` leave 450–520px of empty framed sheet** above the footer at 1440×900. DESIGN.md justifies this as "unused sheet — which is what it is", and the reasoning holds; noting it because it is the largest empty area in the product and reads as broken layout to anyone without the rationale.
- **The language switch is a documented fallback, not a bug** — but the control discloses nothing: on a post with no translation, `Deutsch` points at `/de/` with no visual difference from a page where it points at a real translation. The policy is right; the control should say which of the two it is doing.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the zone rail is the differentiator no neighbouring theme could truthfully copy, why is it set smaller and dimmer than every other element on the sheet — and why does the theme's own cover illustration draw it bigger and bolder than the rail itself? Which of the two is the design?
2. `TocOpen = false` contradicts the theme's own template comment. Was that chosen because the ToC competes visually with the rail? If two navigation systems cannot coexist on one sheet without one being hidden, is the answer to hide one — or to admit the rail is a *position indicator* and the ToC is the *index*, and design them as two different instruments rather than substitutes?
3. DESIGN.md says amber is "never doing more than one job on a screen at a time", and a screen with three amber marks contains an amber box asserting exactly that. Is the rule wrong or are the alerts wrong? Deciding is worth more than either fix.
4. Both binding commitments are script-checkable and both held. Every P0 and P1 here is a property no script was watching: a button's position relative to the text under it, an anchor's landing offset under one media query, a state that is applied but never on screen, a `<link>` to a file that does not exist. What would it take to make "the lit zone is visible at the moment it lights" and "every emitted asset URL returns 200" assertions in `.parity/shots.mjs`, the way route status already is?
5. Twice, the accessible name carries information the visual design withholds: the screen-reader user gets `A, What the title block records` while the sighted user gets `A`; the screen-reader user gets `AI use: With assistance` while the sighted user gets `◇`. Is the visual layer under-specified — or has the accessible name quietly become where the real labels live?
